How the Labs Work: Structure, Individual Tasks, Marking and AWS Academy

A complete walkthrough of the ELEC3609/9609 lab program — 13 weeks of hands-on labs, eight individually marked tasks, in-person marking rules, submission and late penalties, and the optional AWS Academy courses you set up early for the final deployment assignment.

Read this first: The labs are where you earn marks by showing your work in person. You prepare a short PDF and submit it on Canvas, but the tutor awards your mark based on your live explanation or demo during the lab. Everything else in this guide — the schedule, deadlines, AWS setup — exists to make sure you show up prepared to that conversation.

What the Lab Program Actually Is

The lab program is 13 weeks long, split between nine teaching labs and four weeks reserved for project demonstrations and assessment.

There are 13 labs, starting from Week 1. They fall into two distinct groups:

  • 9 core-teaching labs — structured around key technical and practical skills. These are the labs where new material is introduced and practised (e.g. Django, REST APIs, cloud deployment).
  • 4 project/assessment labs — reserved for project demonstrations, assignment assessment, and milestone reviews, giving dedicated time for student presentations and tutor feedback.

Across the semester you complete eight individual tasks that engage you with the core lab content and ensure every team member actively contributes to the group project.

ℹ️
Note: Lab and assignment content is delivered through the course website, with links posted on Canvas. Once a content link is posted or updated on Canvas, that linked version is final and confirmed. Check Canvas regularly for new or updated links.
💡
Why it matters: The eight individual tasks are the personal, non-transferable part of your assessment. The group project can absorb some inconsistency in effort; your individual tasks cannot — they are marked on you, live.

Attendance and Group Formation

You must attend the lab session you are officially enrolled in — attending a different lab does not let you join that session's group.

The attendance rules are strict and carry consequences for your group work:

  • You must attend the lab session you are officially enrolled in, based on your allocated lab code in the university system.
  • Attending a different lab without formal approval does not allow you to form or join a group in that session.
  • If you have a timetable issue, contact the unit coordinator or tutors as soon as possible.
⚠️
Common trap: Turning up to a convenient session that is not your allocated one. You may be able to sit there, but you cannot form or join a group in that session — and group membership is central to the project.

Week 1 Has No On-Site Lab

Week 1 is the one exception to the in-person pattern: there is no on-site lab, but the work is still required.

Due to university scheduling, there is no on-site lab in Week 1. Despite this, students are still required to:

  • Read the Week 1 Lab content.
  • Complete Individual Task 1 as normal.
⚠️
Common trap: Treating a week with no on-site lab as a week off. The Week 1 reading and Individual Task 1 are mandatory; the marking for Task 1 simply happens when labs begin.

Knowledge Checks

Knowledge Checks are optional self-review activities that help you find gaps — they do not count toward your marks.

Knowledge Checks are optional interactive review materials designed to strengthen your understanding of important lab concepts and help you identify topics needing further revision.

Key facts:

  • They are not assessment tasks and do not contribute to your marks.
  • Your answers and results are not submitted to or saved by the teaching team.
  • The activities run locally in your browser using JavaScript.
  • A small completion record may be kept only in your browser's local storage for convenience — it is not uploaded, monitored, or used for assessment, and it may disappear if you clear your browser data or switch devices.
ℹ️
Note: The questions and interactive explanations were created with AI assistance. They have been reviewed, but AI-assisted content may still contain mistakes, ambiguity, or oversimplifications. If an answer seems unclear or wrong, or you want to discuss the reasoning, ask on Ed Discussion.

How Each Lab Runs

Every lab is primarily hands-on and self-guided, with a fixed three-hour rhythm of announcements, marking, and consultation.

Each lab is primarily hands-on and self-guided. Students are expected to:

  • Read and understand the lab content independently.
  • Complete the associated individual task for the lab.
  • Attend in-person labs to have their previous task marked.
  • Engage in Q&A with tutors about the current lab or ongoing project work.

Tutors provide weekly updates, mark individual submissions in-lab, and answer questions.

The standard lab timeline (3 hours)

TimeActivity
0:10 – 0:30Tutors make announcements (e.g. assignment releases), summarise the key points from the previous lab, and introduce the next individual task, highlighting important expectations and potential pitfalls.
0:30 – 2:00Tutors mark each student's individual task submission from the previous lab. Each student gets around 4 minutes to explain or demo their work. Meanwhile you can work on the individual task, read the week's lab guide, or discuss your group project.
2:00 – 3:00Remaining time is open for questions and consultation — current lab tasks, project progress, assignment preparation, or technical support.
✅
Exam tip: The first 20 minutes (0:10–0:30) are where the next task and its pitfalls are explained. Arrive on time; missing this briefing is how students lose easy marks on expectations.

Pre-lab Preparation

Before each lab you review the previous content, submit the previous task, and optionally preview the upcoming lab guide.

Before attending each lab you should:

  1. Review the previous lab content thoroughly.
  2. Complete and submit the previous week's individual task on Canvas before the deadline.
  3. (Optional) Read the current lab guide in advance so you can ask specific questions during the lab.
💡
Why it matters: Marking in week N covers the task from week N−1. If you have not submitted and understood it, the 4-minute in-lab marking slot becomes a conversation you cannot lead.

The Eight Individual Tasks

There are eight individual tasks (Lab 01–08), each worth 1%, released with their lab and due the following Wednesday midnight.

There are eight individual tasks (Lab 01–08), each worth 1%. Each task is:

  • Released together with the corresponding lab.
  • Due by the following Wednesday midnight — for example, the Lab 1 task is due on Week 2 Wednesday night.

The Week 4 exception

⚠️
Common trap: The Lab 4 task (Week 4) is due in Week 6, not the following Wednesday, because Week 5 is allocated for the A1 Milestone Presentation.

If you are unsure about a deadline, always refer to the Canvas assignment deadline, which is considered final.

🧠
Memory hook: Deadline default = next Wednesday midnight. The single exception = Lab 4 slips to Week 6 because A1 occupies Week 5.

What to Submit and How Much to Write

Submit a single PDF on Canvas that follows the task guideline — around five pages is plenty.

Submission rules:

  • Submit a single PDF report via Canvas.
  • The format is flexible but must follow the task guideline provided in the lab.
  • For technical tasks (e.g. coding, diagrams), it is acceptable to include screenshots.

How much do I need to write?

  • There is no fixed word or page count, and you do not need to write more than necessary.
  • Your report should clearly address the required discussion and provide sufficient evidence — code snippets, diagrams, or screenshots.
  • Five pages should be quite enough.
  • The PDF supports what you explain during the lab; it does not replace the in-person explanation or demonstration.
✅
Exam tip: Treat the PDF as your demo script, not your essay. Every claim in it should be something you can point to and explain live.

How Individual Tasks Are Marked

Marks come from your live explanation or demo in the week the task is due — the PDF is only supporting evidence.

Marking rules:

  • Tutors mark your work in person during the lab, in the week the task is due.
  • You must present, explain, or demo your work (as documented in your report), and there may be some short Q&A during the process.
  • Canvas submission is required and is reviewed as supporting evidence, but marks are awarded based on your live explanation/demo during the lab.

Two ways to lose marks despite a good report

  1. If your report includes screenshots or explanations but you fail to cover those in your live demo, you may still lose marks.
  2. If your explanation during the lab has significant inconsistencies with your submitted report, you will also lose marks.

Prepare your report, notes, or demo project ahead of time to support your in-lab explanation.

Late Submission Penalties

Late written work is penalised 5% of the maximum mark per calendar day, with zero after ten days.

In accordance with University policy, these penalties apply when written work is submitted after 11:59pm on the due date:

  • Deduction of 5% of the maximum mark for each calendar day after the due date.
  • After ten calendar days late, a mark of zero will be awarded.
⚠️
Common trap: Assuming the in-lab marking protects you from late penalties. The Canvas submission deadline is separate and enforced; submit the PDF on time even if your demo is later.

AWS Academy: What It Is and Why Set It Up Early

You are invited to two AWS Academy courses and the AWS Learner Lab — optional and not examined, but needed for the final deployment task.

All students have been invited to join two AWS Academy courses and the AWS Learner Lab through the AWS Academy platform.

Why it matters:

  • The AWS Learner Lab environment will be used later in the semester for your deployment task in the final assignment, so it is essential that you ensure access as early as possible.
  • The two AWS Academy courses are not mandatory and will not be examined in the final exam, but they are highly recommended.
  • They offer a comprehensive and structured introduction to cloud computing architecture, helping you understand how internet platforms are built and deployed.
  • Upon completion you receive two official AWS Academy Certificates.

Check your university email for invitations to the AWS Academy resources, and use the email that received the invitation (typically your Unikey email) to register.

💡
Why it matters: Access problems are cheapest to fix early. If you discover an invitation or login issue the week of the deployment task, you have lost valuable time.

The Two AWS Academy Courses

Cloud Foundations builds core AWS knowledge; Cloud Architecting goes deeper into scalable, secure design and maps to a certification.

The two provided courses are:

  1. AWS Academy Cloud Foundations
  • Covers core AWS services and concepts such as compute, storage, and networking.
  • Ideal for beginners building foundational knowledge of cloud platforms.
  1. AWS Academy Cloud Architecting
  • Dives deeper into scalable and secure cloud solution design.
  • Aligned with the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate certification.
🧠
Memory hook: Foundations = the pieces (compute, storage, networking). Architecting = putting the pieces together at scale, securely.

Accessing the AWS Learner Lab (and Staying Safe)

First-time access requires an AWS Academy Canvas account — which is not your USYD Canvas account — and you should never enter personal or payment details.

Two documents are provided on Canvas to guide you:

  • AWS Academy Learner Lab – Student Guide
  • AWS Academy Learner Lab – README (usage rules and expectations)

When accessing AWS Academy for the first time via an invitation link, you will be prompted to a sign-in page. You can either:

  • Sign in using your existing AWS Academy Canvas account (note: not your USYD Canvas account), or
  • Create a new account by selecting "Create My Account." When creating a new account, you only need to set a password — no additional information is required.

Follow the steps in the Student Guide carefully. If you have not received an invitation or hit any access issues, ask your tutor for help during the lab.

⚠️
Danger — stay safe: You should never be asked to provide personal information such as your university account password or credit card details. If you encounter such requests, contact tutors immediately.
