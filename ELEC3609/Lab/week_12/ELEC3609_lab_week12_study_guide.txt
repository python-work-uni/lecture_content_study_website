ELEC3609 Lab Week 12 – Study Guide
A2 Technical Interview · Lab 12
================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
In Week 12 your group takes part in a **technical interview** conducted by the tutors, focused on your **Assignment 2 (A2)** source code, system architecture and development process. This is an assessment, not a presentation to the class: tutors visit your table and ask questions individually. The interview contributes to your final project mark and is designed to test your **technical understanding and individual contributions**.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: A2 Technical Interview Overview
------------------------------------------------
Each group will participate in a technical interview conducted by the tutors, focusing on your A2 **source code, system architecture and development process**.

This lab session is a **technical interview** to assess your understanding of your implementation (Assignment 2). Tutors will visit each group and ask questions.

💡 **Why It Matters:** The interview checks whether you genuinely understand what you built. It rewards teams who can explain and justify their own code, not just teams whose code happens to run.

ℹ️ **Background:** You should use the materials you submitted in **Week 11** — your source code and report — as the basis for the discussion.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: What Tutors Will Ask About
------------------------------------------------
Tutors will ask questions about:
- Your **project architecture** and design choices.
- **Code structure** and key implementation decisions.
- The content in your submitted **assignment report**.
- Any **technical challenges or trade-offs** encountered.

You should be prepared to **explain, justify and walk through** parts of your system using your submitted materials from Week 11 (source code and report).

🧠 **Memory Hook:** Four question areas — **A-C-R-T** — "Architecture, Code structure, Report, Trade-offs."

⚠️ **Common Trap:** Memorising a script about the project in general, then being unable to answer a question about a specific file or design decision. Prepare to go deep on your own code.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: Interview Order and Timing
------------------------------------------------
- Groups will be interviewed in **ascending group number order** (for example Group 1 → Group 2 → Group 3…).
- Each group is allocated **10 minutes total** for the interview.
- You are expected to spend roughly:
  - **~5 minutes** introducing your architecture and key implementation points.
  - **~5 minutes** responding to questions tailored to your project.

ℹ️ **Info:** If your group needs to be interviewed earlier because of urgent circumstances, please notify the tutors **before the lab begins**.

🧠 **Memory Hook:** Ten minutes = **5 + 5** — half presenting your architecture, half answering questions.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 4: What to Have Ready
------------------------------------------------
Ensure your group has:
- A **running version of your project** (local only).
- Your **assignment report** and **source code** ready to access.

All group members must attend, and everyone should be ready to answer.

💡 **Why It Matters:** The project is expected to run **locally only** — you do not need a live deployment for the A2 interview. What matters is that the app runs, the key features are accessible, and you can navigate your own codebase quickly.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: Conduct During the Interview
------------------------------------------------
- Groups will **not present to the whole lab**. Tutors will visit your table and conduct the interview **individually**.
- Keep the required materials — running project, report and source code — open and ready.
- Be **respectful and quiet** while other groups are being interviewed.
- **All group members must attend**, and everyone should be ready to answer.

⚠️ **Common Trap:** Letting one member answer everything. The interview focuses on individual contributions, so each member should be able to speak to their part.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: How It Counts and How to Prepare
------------------------------------------------
This interview **contributes to your final project mark** and focuses on your **technical understanding and individual contributions**.

To prepare:
- **Practice explaining** your system architecture and logic.
- Ensure your app **runs correctly** and key features are accessible.
- **Use your report and submitted codebase** to support your answers.

🧠 **Memory Hook:** Preparation is **P-R-U** — "Practice explaining, Run the app, Use your report and code."

================================================
TERMS TO KNOW
================================================
**Technical interview**: A tutor-led, question-and-answer assessment of your understanding of the project.
**A2**: Assignment 2 — the implementation being assessed in this interview.
**Project architecture**: The high-level structure of your system and how its parts fit together.
**Design choices**: The decisions made about how the system is built and why.
**Code structure**: How the codebase is organised (apps, modules, files, classes, functions).
**Implementation decisions**: Specific technical choices made while building features.
**Assignment report**: The written A2 document describing the project.
**Technical challenge**: A difficulty encountered during development.
**Trade-off**: A decision that gains one benefit at the cost of another; you should be able to justify these.
**Source code**: The project codebase used to support your answers.
**Ascending group number order**: Interview order from the lowest group number upward.
**Running version (local only)**: A working copy of the project on your own machine; no live deployment is required for A2.
**Individual contributions**: The work each group member personally did; the interview focuses on this.
**Tutors**: The teaching staff who conduct the interviews and award marks.
**Final project mark**: The overall project grade that this interview contributes to.

WORKED EXAMPLES
================================================
### Example 1: Planning the ten minutes
A group decides in advance that the first five minutes will cover the system architecture: the Django project layout, the main apps, how data flows from the API to the database, and the headline features. The remaining five minutes are reserved for tutor questions. They rehearse the architecture talk twice so it fits the time without rushing.

### Example 2: Explaining an architecture decision
A tutor asks why the team split the project into separate Django apps. A good answer explains the decision and its consequence: each app groups related models, views and serializers, which keeps the codebase organised and makes ownership across team members clearer. The answer references the actual project structure rather than a generic textbook definition.

### Example 3: Answering a trade-off question
A tutor asks why the team used one approach rather than another. A strong answer names the trade-off honestly — for example, choosing a simpler option that was faster to build and easier for the team to maintain, while acknowledging what it gives up — instead of claiming there were no downsides.

### Example 4: Why the project is "local only"
A student worries that A2 requires a live public URL. It does not. The Week 12 requirement is a **running version of your project (local only)**. The student instead makes sure the app starts cleanly on their laptop, the key features work, and the report and source code are open in separate windows ready to reference.
