# **Lab Overview** 

## **A. Lab Overview** 

There are 13 labs starting from Week 1. Among these, 9 labs are structured around core teaching topics, focusing on key technical and practical skills. The remaining 4 labs are allocated for project demonstrations, assignment assessment, and milestone reviews, providing dedicated time for student presentations and tutor feedback. 

There are **eight individual tasks** designed to engage students with the core lab content and ensure that each team member is actively contributing to the group project. Each individual task, along with parts of the assignments, will be **marked in-lab (on-site, face-to-face)** . You must be present in person to receive marks. 

##### **INFO** 

Lab and assignment content is provided through this website, with the relevant links posted on Canvas. Once a content link is posted or updated on Canvas, the linked version is considered final and confirmed. Check Canvas regularly for new or updated links. 

##### **LAB ATTENDANCE AND GROUP FORMATION** 

You must attend the **lab session you're officially enrolled in** , based on your allocated lab code in the university system. 

Attending a different lab without formal approval does **not** allow you to form or join a group in that session. 

If you have a timetable issue, please contact the **unit coordinator or tutors** as soon as possible. 



##### **WEEK 1: NO ON-SITE LAB** 

Due to university scheduling, there is no on-site lab in **Week 1** . Students are still **required** to read the **<u>Week 1 Lab</u>** content and complete **<u>Individual Task 1</u>** as normal.

## **B. Lab Activity** 

Each lab is primarily hands-on and self-guided. Students are expected to: 

- Read and understand the lab content independently. 

- Complete the associated individual task for the lab. 

- Attend in-person labs to have their previous task marked. 

- Engage in Q&A with tutors regarding the current lab or ongoing project work. 

Tutors will provide weekly updates, mark individual submissions in-lab, and address student questions. 

### **B.1 Pre-lab Preparation** 

Before attending each lab, you should: 

1. **Review the previous lab content thoroughly.** 

2. **Complete and submit the previous week's individual task on Canvas before the deadline.** 

3. **(Optional)** Read the current lab guide in advance so that you can ask specific questions during the lab.

##### **HOW MUCH DO I NEED TO WRITE?** 

There is no fixed word or page count, and you do not need to write more than necessary. Your report should clearly address the required discussion and provide sufficient evidence, such as code snippets, diagrams, or screenshots. **Five pages should be quite enough** . The PDF supports what you explain during the lab; it does not replace the inperson explanation or demonstration. 

#### **How We Mark** 

- Tutors will mark your work **in-person during the lab** in the **week the task is due** . 

- You must **present, explain, or demo** your work (as documented in your report), and there may be some short **Q&A** during the process. 

- While Canvas submission is required and will be reviewed as supporting evidence, **marks are awarded based on your live explanation/demo** during the lab. 

##### **BE READY FOR MARKING** 

If your report includes screenshots or explanations but you fail to cover those in your live demo, **you may still lose marks** . 

If your explanation during the lab has **significant inconsistencies** with your submitted report, you will also **lose marks** . 

Prepare your report, notes, or demo project ahead of time to support your in-lab explanation.
