================================================
ELEC3609 Lab Overview – Study Guide
Lab Stream Overview & Assessments
================================================

OVERVIEW
--------
The ELEC3609/9609 lab stream is a hands-on, self-guided program of 13 weekly labs that builds the practical skills behind the group project. Nine labs teach core technical topics — planning, GitHub collaboration, Django, DRF, security, AWS/GraphQL and deployment — while four are reserved for assessment, demonstrations and milestone reviews. This guide explains how the labs run, when individual tasks are due, how your work is marked, and how to get access to the AWS Academy resources you will need for the final deployment.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 1: Lab Stream at a Glance
------------------------------------------------
The lab stream starts in **Week 1** and runs for **13 weeks**. Of those 13 labs, **9 are structured around core teaching topics** that focus on key technical and practical skills. The remaining **4 labs are allocated for project demonstrations, assignment assessment and milestone reviews**, giving dedicated time for student presentations and tutor feedback.

There are **eight individual tasks** designed to engage you with the core lab content and to make sure every team member is actively contributing to the group project. Each individual task, together with parts of the assignments, is **marked in-lab (on-site, face-to-face)** — you must be present in person to receive marks.

💡 **Why It Matters:** The lab is where your group project gets checked and marked. Attending in person is not optional if you want the marks for the work you have already done.

ℹ️ **Background:** Lab and assignment content is provided through the lab website, with the relevant links posted on Canvas. Once a content link is posted or updated on Canvas, the linked version is considered final and confirmed — check Canvas regularly for new or updated links.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 2: Attendance and Group Formation
------------------------------------------------
- **Attend your enrolled session** — You must attend the lab session you are officially enrolled in, based on the lab code allocated to you in the university system.
- **No swapping without approval** — Attending a different lab without formal approval does **not** allow you to form or join a group in that session.
- **Timetable clashes** — If you have a timetable issue, contact the unit coordinator or your tutors as soon as possible.

⚠️ **Common Trap:** Turning up to a different lab and expecting to join a group there. Without formal approval the attendance does not count for group formation.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3: Week 1: No On-Site Lab
------------------------------------------------
Because of university scheduling, there is **no on-site lab in Week 1**. Students are still **required** to read the **Week 1 Lab** content and complete **Individual Task 1** as normal.

💡 **Why It Matters:** Week 1 sets up the planning and design skills used by every later lab. Skipping it because there is no class puts you behind from the first deadline.

SECTION 5: How Each Lab Runs
------------------------------------------------
Each lab is primarily **hands-on and self-guided**. Students are expected to:
- Read and understand the lab content independently.
- Complete the associated individual task for the lab.
- Attend in-person labs to have their previous task marked.
- Engage in Q&A with tutors regarding the current lab or ongoing project work.

Tutors provide weekly updates, mark individual submissions in-lab, and address student questions. The 3-hour session follows a predictable structure:

| Time | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0:10 – 0:30 | Tutors make announcements (e.g. assignment releases), summarise the **key points** from the previous lab, and **introduce the next individual task**, highlighting important expectations and potential pitfalls. |
| 0:30 – 2:00 | Tutors **mark each student's individual task submission** from the previous lab. Each student gets around **4 minutes** to explain or demo their work. Meanwhile you can work on the individual task, read the lab guide, or discuss your group project. |
| 2:00 – 3:00 | Remaining time is open for **questions and consultation** — current lab tasks, project progress, assignment preparation, or technical support. |

💡 **Why It Matters:** Because marking happens in the first 90 minutes, arrive ready — with your previous task open, your evidence handy and a short explanation rehearsed.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 6: Pre-Lab Preparation
------------------------------------------------
Before attending each lab, you should:
1. **Review the previous lab content thoroughly.**
2. **Complete and submit the previous week's individual task on Canvas before the deadline.**
3. **(Optional) Read the current lab guide in advance** so you can ask specific questions during the lab.

📝 **Tip:** Reading the current guide ahead of time turns the tutor Q&A window into a focused debugging session instead of a first read-through.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 7: Individual Tasks & Deadlines
------------------------------------------------
There are **eight individual tasks (Lab 01–08)**, each worth **1%**. Every task is:
- Released together with the corresponding lab.
- Due **by the following Wednesday midnight** (for example, the Lab 1 task is due Week 2 on Wednesday night).

⚠️ **Exception:** The **Lab 4 task (Week 4) is due in Week 6**, because Week 5 is allocated for the A1 Milestone Presentation.

If you are unsure about a deadline, always refer to the **Canvas assignment deadline**, which is considered final.

🧠 **Memory Hook:** Default rule = **"next Wednesday midnight."** The single exception = **"Lab 4 → Week 6."**

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 8: What to Submit and How Much
------------------------------------------------
- Submit a **single PDF report** via Canvas.
- The format is flexible but must **follow the task guideline** provided in the lab.
- For technical tasks (e.g. coding, diagrams) it is acceptable to include **screenshots** in your report.
- There is **no fixed word or page count**, and you do not need to write more than necessary. Your report should clearly address the required discussion and provide sufficient evidence such as code snippets, diagrams or screenshots. **Five pages should be quite enough.**
- The PDF **supports** what you explain during the lab; it does **not replace** the in-person explanation or demonstration.

💡 **Why It Matters:** The report is evidence, not the assessment itself. Write enough to prove the work, then be ready to talk through it.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 9: How Your Work Is Marked
------------------------------------------------
- Tutors mark your work **in-person during the lab in the week the task is due**.
- You must **present, explain or demo** your work (as documented in your report), and there may be some short **Q&A** during the process.
- Canvas submission is required and will be reviewed as supporting evidence, but **marks are awarded based on your live explanation/demo** during the lab.

⚠️ **Be ready for marking:** If your report includes screenshots or explanations but you fail to cover those in your live demo, you may still **lose marks**. If your explanation during the lab has **significant inconsistencies** with your submitted report, you will also **lose marks**. Prepare your report, notes or demo project ahead of time.

| Deliverable | Format | Submission Platform | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Report | PDF | Canvas | Submit before the deadline; used as proof of work. |
| In-Lab Marking | N/A | In Lab | Marking is based on what you show or explain. |

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 10: Late Submission Penalties
------------------------------------------------
In accordance with University policy, these penalties apply when written work is submitted after **11:59pm on the due date**:
- Deduction of **5% of the maximum mark for each calendar day** after the due date.
- After **ten calendar days late**, a mark of **zero** will be awarded.

⚠️ **Common Trap:** Believing the Canvas upload alone earns the mark. Marks are awarded in the lab, so a late upload plus a missed demo means you lose the mark regardless.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 11: Knowledge Checks (Optional)
------------------------------------------------
**Knowledge Checks** are optional interactive review materials designed to strengthen your understanding of important lab concepts and help you identify topics that may need further revision.
- They are **not assessment tasks** and do not contribute to your marks.
- Your answers and results are **not submitted to or saved by the teaching team** — the activities run locally in your browser using JavaScript.
- A small completion record may be kept only in your browser's **local storage** for convenience; it is not uploaded, monitored or used for assessment, and may disappear if you clear your browser data or use another device.

ℹ️ **AI-assisted content:** The questions and interactive explanations were created with AI assistance. Although they have been reviewed, AI-assisted content may still contain mistakes, ambiguity or oversimplifications. If an answer seems unclear or incorrect, ask on **Ed Discussion**.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 12: AWS Academy & Courses
------------------------------------------------
All students have been invited to join **two AWS Academy courses** and the **AWS Learner Lab** through the AWS Academy platform. The AWS Learner Lab environment will be used later in the semester for **your deployment task in the final assignment**, so it is essential to ensure access as early as possible.

- While the two AWS Academy courses are **not mandatory** and will **not be examined** in the final exam, they are highly recommended — they offer a comprehensive, structured introduction to cloud computing architecture.
- On completion you receive **two official AWS Academy Certificates**.
- Check your **university email** for invitations, and use the email that received the invitation (typically your Unikey email) to register.

**The two provided courses:**
1. **AWS Academy Cloud Foundations** — covers core AWS services and concepts such as compute, storage and networking; ideal for beginners building foundational cloud knowledge.
2. **AWS Academy Cloud Architecting** — dives deeper into scalable and secure cloud solution design; aligned with the AWS Certified Solutions Architect – Associate certification.

💡 **Why It Matters:** The Learner Lab is the actual environment for your final deployment. Sorting out access early avoids losing lab time later when the deployment deadline is close.

------------------------------------------------
SECTION 13: Accessing the Learner Lab
------------------------------------------------
Two documents are provided on **Canvas** to guide you:
- **AWS Academy Learner Lab – Student Guide**
- **AWS Academy Learner Lab – README** (usage rules and expectations)

When accessing AWS Academy for the first time via an invitation link, you will be prompted to a sign-in page. You can either sign in using your existing **AWS Academy Canvas account** (note: this is **not** your USYD Canvas account) or create a new account by selecting **"Create My Account"**. When creating a new account you only need to set a password, without providing any additional information.

📝 **Tip:** If you have never used AWS Academy before, create the **AWS Academy (Canvas)** account first — it is a different system from USYD Canvas. Follow the steps in the Student Guide carefully. If you have not received an invitation or hit access issues, ask your tutor for help during the lab.

⚠️ **Danger:** You should **not** be asked to provide any personal information such as your university account password or credit card details. If you encounter such requests, contact tutors immediately.

================================================
WORKED EXAMPLES
================================================
### Example 1: Working out when a task is due
A student starts Lab 1 in Week 1. The task is released that week and is due by the **following Wednesday midnight**, i.e. Week 2. The same pattern continues for Labs 1–3 and 5–8. The **only exception** is the Lab 4 task (released Week 4), which is due in **Week 6** because Week 5 is the A1 Milestone Presentation. If any of this is unclear, the student checks the Canvas assignment deadline, which is final.

### Example 2: What a five-page report contains
For a technical task, a student submits: a short statement of what was built, a diagram or screenshot of the working feature, relevant code snippets, and a brief explanation of each. The report clearly addresses the required discussion and stays within about five pages. It is written to support a live explanation, not to replace it — the student will still demo the running feature in the lab.

### Example 3: Calculating a late penalty
A 1% individual task is submitted **3 calendar days late**. The penalty is 5% of the maximum mark per day: 3 × 5% = **15% of the maximum mark**. If the task's maximum is 1 mark, 0.15 is deducted. If the same task were 11 days late, it would score **zero**, because the zero rule applies after ten calendar days.

### Example 4: A marking inconsistency
A student's report contains a screenshot of a working login system and a written explanation of how authentication was implemented. During the in-lab demo the student cannot log in and cannot explain the flow. Because the live explanation does not match the submitted report, the student risks losing marks even though the PDF "looked complete."
