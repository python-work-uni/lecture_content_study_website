01

What the A1 Milestone Is

This milestone is a group presentation where every group gives a short pitch of its proposed project idea to the class and tutors. The audience includes both the teaching team and your peers. The stated goal is to impress the audience with the merits and potential of your future web application. After each pitch there is a brief Q&A session with tutors (and possibly other teams), which is offered as valuable feedback and ideas for improving your project.

💡
Why It Matters:: The milestone is an early checkpoint. It forces you to sharpen what your project is and who it helps before you invest weeks building it, and it gives you free expert feedback you can fold into later sprints.
ℹ️
Background:: This presentation contributes to your final assignment mark, so treat it as assessed work rather than an informal chat.
02

The 5-Minute Elevator Pitch

In this milestone presentation, each group delivers a 5-minute elevator pitch introducing the core concept and innovation of the proposed website project. The instruction is to focus only on the core value of the project — what makes it interesting, unique, and valuable to your target users. The 5-minute format is what makes it an "elevator pitch": short enough that you must ruthlessly prioritise the single best idea rather than list every feature.

DoDon't
Introduce the core concept and innovationWander through every planned feature
Explain why it is interesting, unique and valuable to usersGive a generic description any project could use
Aim to impress with merits and potentialRead your script word for word
✅
Exam Tip:: A script is allowed, but reading directly from it will significantly reduce your mark. Use the script as a rehearsal aid and safety net, not as a teleprompter.
03

What to Cover: Core Value

The pitch should make four things unmistakably clear:

  • Core concept — what your website fundamentally is.
  • Innovation — what is new or distinctive about it.
  • Target users — who it is for.
  • Value / merits — why it is interesting, unique and valuable to those users.

The assessment reminder reinforces a fifth, critical element: how your design addresses the needs of your users. That is the thread tutors will listen for — not just a clever idea, but a clever idea that solves a real user need.

💡
Why It Matters:: "Core value" is the test of every sentence in your pitch. If a sentence does not support the concept, the innovation, the users, or their needs, it probably does not belong in five minutes.
🧠
Memory Hook:: Pitch the C-I-V-U — Concept, Innovation, Value, Users — then close by connecting your design to user needs.
04

Running Order & Timing

Groups present in ascending group number order (for example, Group 1 → Group 2 → Group 3, and so on).

📌
Logistics the brief specifies::
  • Be ready two groups ahead of your scheduled turn.
  • If your group needs to present earlier due to urgent circumstances, notify the tutors before the lab begins.
  • Each group has 5 minutes for the presentation, followed by a short Q&A.
  • There is a strict time limit. Any group will be stopped when time is up.
  • The timer will be enforced to ensure fairness.
⚠️
Common Trap:: Turning up "just in time" for your slot. If the groups ahead run quickly, your turn can arrive sooner than the schedule suggests, so the brief asks you to be ready two groups ahead.
05

During the Session

Behaviour during the session is part of the milestone expectations:

  • Be respectful and quiet during other groups' presentations.
  • All group members must attend.
  • Not all members need to speak, but everyone should be ready to answer questions.
  • Absence or failure to reasonably answer questions may result in mark penalties.
💡
Why It Matters:: The Q&A is open to any group member. Even if one person delivers the pitch, the whole group is expected to understand the project well enough to defend it, so everyone should prepare.
06

How You Are Assessed

The brief states the presentation:

  • Contributes to your final assignment mark.
  • Is assessed on how effectively you communicate the value and goals of your proposed application, and particularly how your design addresses the needs of your users.

It also advises you to practice in advance. A script may be used, but reading directly from it will significantly reduce your mark.

⚠️
Common Trap:: Treating this as a technical demo. The marking emphasis is on communication and user-need fit, not on how much code you can describe.
✅
Exam Tip:: Rehearse so the delivery sounds spoken, not read. Structure your talk around value and user needs, then use the Q&A to show the whole group's depth.

Worked Scenarios & Calculations

Scenario 1 • Structuring a 5-Minute

Structuring a 5-Minute Core-Value Pitch

The brief does not prescribe a slide-by-slide structure, but it does name the parts that must come through. A plan that covers all of them:

  1. Core concept (open) — say what the site is in one clear sentence.
  2. Innovation — explain what is new or distinctive about it.
  3. Target users — name who it is for.
  4. Value and merits — why it is interesting and valuable to those users.
  5. Design → needs (close) — show how your design addresses their needs.

Because the limit is 5 minutes and the timer is enforced, keep each part tight; the closing link from design to user needs is the part the brief singles out for assessment.

Scenario 2 • Caught by the Strict Timer

Caught by the Strict Timer

A group plans a relaxed 8-minute talk. Around the 5-minute mark the enforced timer stops them mid-slide. Their innovation and user-need link are never delivered, and the pitch ends on an awkward cut. The lesson: time the pitch to 5 minutes in rehearsal, because the timer "will be enforced to ensure fairness."

Scenario 3 • The "Reading the Script" Trap

The "Reading the Script" Trap

A student writes a full script and reads it aloud. The brief warns that reading directly from a script will significantly reduce the mark, even though a script is allowed. A stronger approach is to rehearse until the script is internalised, then deliver the pitch conversationally and keep the script only as a backup — the mark rewards communicating value and goals.
