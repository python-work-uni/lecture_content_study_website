Lecture overview

Week 11 is a Project Help & Review Session, not a teaching lab and not an assessed demonstration. There is no in-person project demonstration in this lab. The time is reserved for teams to review their Assignment 2 work and ask tutors for help before submission, and to prepare for the Week 12 technical interview. Bring your current source code, your report draft and a list of specific questions.

01

Session Purpose

This lab is a Project Help & Review Session. There is no in-person project demonstration here.

The session is reserved for teams to:

  • Review their Assignment 2 work.
  • Ask tutors for help before submission.
💡

Why It Matters:

This is the last scheduled opportunity to get tutor feedback before A2 is submitted. Treat it as a rehearsal and a debugging window, not a free period.
ℹ️

Background:

The session is explicitly not an assessed presentation. It exists to help you improve and finish the assignment, and to prepare for the interview that follows in Week 12.
02

What to Do in the Lab

Use the lab time to:

  • Clarify the Assignment 2 requirements and the submission process.
  • Ask questions about implementation, testing, documentation, or the recorded demonstration video.
  • Troubleshoot remaining technical issues with guidance from a tutor.
  • Review whether your report and video provide sufficient evidence for the features you implemented.
  • Prepare for the Week 12 technical interview.
🧠

Memory Hook:

Five uses of the session — C-A-T-R-P — "Clarify, Ask, Troubleshoot, Review, Prepare."
⚠️

Common Trap:

Showing up with the code but no questions. The session works best when you arrive with a specific list of blockers or uncertainties.
03

What to Bring

Bring your:

  • Current source code — so a tutor can look at the actual implementation.
  • Report draft — so feedback can target what you have written so far.
  • List of specific questions — so the limited time is spent on the things only a tutor can resolve.
📝

Tip:

A written list of questions keeps the session focused and makes sure you leave with answers to everything important before submission.
04

Tutor Help: Limits and Scope

Tutors can provide guidance and feedback during this session.

However:

  • The session is not an assessed presentation.
  • Tutors will not complete or pre-mark the assignment for you.

For the deliverables and the submission deadline, refer to the Assignment 2 requirements.

⚠️

Common Trap:

Expecting tutors to check your work feature-by-feature as if it were marking. They can guide you, but the assignment must be your own work and the official requirements remain the source of truth.
05

Preparing for Week 12

One listed purpose of this session is to prepare for the Week 12 technical interview.

Because the session is dedicated to reviewing A2, the material you bring here — your source code, your report draft and your understanding of what you implemented — is exactly the material you will need to discuss in Week 12.

💡

Why It Matters:

Walking a tutor through your architecture and evidence now is the best rehearsal for the interview. The gaps you discover here can be fixed before the interview, not during it.

Worked Examples & Scenarios

Example 1

Turning a vague worry into a specific question

A team writes down "authentication feels wrong." That is too vague for a short session. They rewrite it as three specific questions: which permission classes should protect the endpoint, whether the recorded video shows the login and logout flow, and whether the report explains how tokens are stored. The tutor can now answer each one in the lab time.

Example 2

Checking evidence for a feature

A team implemented file uploads. In their report draft the feature is described in one sentence, and their demonstration video does not show it at all. During review they realise the evidence is not sufficient, so they rerecord that section of the video and expand the report's explanation before submission.

Example 3

A productive versus unproductive session

An unproductive use of the session: arriving with no code, no draft and no questions, and waiting for the tutor to tell you what to do. A productive use: opening the running project, pointing at a failing test, showing the relevant part of the report, and asking whether the explanation is clear enough. The second approach uses the available time to remove real blockers.
